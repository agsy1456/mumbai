Vadapav

  • Vada pav is a famous fast-food dish that originally started in Mumbai, Maharashtra.
  • People often call it the "Indian burger" because of how it looks.
  • The word "vada" means a spicy mashed potato fritter coated in gram flour batter.
  • The word "pav" comes from the Portuguese word for a soft bread bun or roll.
  • It is usually served with spicy green chutney, sweet tamarind chutney, and dry garlic chutney.
  • A fried green chili pepper is often served on the side for extra spice.

Shawrma Roll

  • Vada shawarma is an innovative street food item merging Indian and Middle Eastern cuisines.
  • It replaces standard roasted chicken or meat with a crispy, savory South Indian lentil or potato vada.
  • The hot and crunchy vada is gently crushed or sliced before being placed inside the wrap.
  • Soft flatbread or rumali roti serves as the outer roll, similar to a classic shawarma wrap.
  • It is generously layered with creamy garlic mayonnaise or traditional toum sauce.
  • Fresh crunchy salad vegetables like cabbage, onions, and cucumbers add a refreshing crunch.

Ragda

  • Ragda is a popular and flavorful curry or gravy base used extensively in Indian street food culture.
  • The core ingredient of this dish is dried white peas, locally known as safed matar or safed vatana.
  • To prepare it, the dried peas must be soaked overnight for 8 to 9 hours before cooking.
  • The soaked peas are pressure-cooked until they become soft, tender, and slightly mushy.
  • It is seasoned with aromatic spices such as turmeric, red chili powder, cumin, and asafoetida.
  • After cooking, the peas are lightly mashed to give the gravy a thick, creamy consistency.

Pavbhaji

  • Pav bhaji is a very popular fast food and street food in India.
  • The dish originated in Mumbai in the state of Maharashtra.
  • It was originally created as a quick, light lunch for textile mill workers.
  • "Bhaji" is a spicy, mashed vegetable curry cooked with a special spice blend.
  • The curry contains healthy vegetables like potatoes, peas, tomatoes, and cauliflower

Falooda

  • Falooda is a popular and refreshing cold dessert beverage that originated in the Indian subcontinent.
  • It is traditionally made by layering several delicious ingredients in a tall glass.
  • The key components include rose syrup, vermicelli noodles, and chewy sweet basil seeds (sabja).
  • Milk and a scoop of rich vanilla or kulfi ice cream are added to give it a creamy texture.
  • It is often garnished with chopped nuts like almonds, pistachios, and cashews for an extra crunch.
  • The dessert has ancient roots, having evolved from a Persian dish called faloodeh.

Bhelpuri

  • Bhelpuri is a famous, crispy Indian street food.
  • It belongs to the chaat family of savory snacks.
  • The main base is crunchy puffed rice, known as murmura.
  • It is mixed with crisp fried sev and crushed puris.
  • Finely chopped onions, tomatoes, and boiled potatoes are added.
  • Tangy tamarind chutney and spicy green chutney give it flavor.
  • A squeeze of fresh lemon juice adds a zesty kick.
  • It offers a mix of sweet, sour, salty, and spicy tastes.
